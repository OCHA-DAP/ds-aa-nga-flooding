New Google model: re-selecting the gauges

The comparison page keeps the ten endorsed gauges and refits only their levels. Here the design's gauge selection is re-run on the new model as well, exactly as it was run on the old one, and the endorsed rule (at least 6 of 10 gauges over their own 1-in-4 level on the same day) is applied to the ten gauges it picks. All comparisons use the years both model versions cover: the record 1998–2023 and forecasts 2016–2022. Analysis of 4 November 2026.

1What changes

  • The gauges. None of the ten endorsed gauges make the new top ten. The new ten sit further down the Benue: 7 downstream of Numan, where the Gongola joins, and 3 between Numan and Yola. All ten endorsed gauges sit upstream of them, from about 15 km below Yola to above it, and rank 12 to 29 on the new model.
  • The ranking is almost flat. On the new model the top 15 candidates all correlate with FloodScan between 0.757 and 0.765, and the tenth and eleventh differ by only 0.0004, so which ten are picked comes down to the fourth decimal. On the old model the top ten ranged from 0.711 to 0.742. The new model also matches FloodScan best with no time offset at every one of these fifteen gauges.
  • Activation years. 1998, 1999, 2012, 2018, 2020, 2022 with the re-selected gauges, against 1998, 1999, 2012, 2018, 2019, 2022 with the endorsed gauges (the same on either model). 2019, a FloodScan 1-in-3 year, drops out; 2020, below the FloodScan 1-in-3 level, comes in. Against the design's 1-in-4 benchmark the scores are unchanged; against 1-in-3 there is one hit fewer and one false alarm more.
  • Timing. First day the rule is met, re-selected gauges against the old design: same day in 1998, 7 days earlier in 1999, 23 days later in 2012, 1 day earlier in 2018, 5 days earlier in 2022.
  • Forecasts (complete seasons 2016–2022): the re-selected gauges meet the rule in 2018, 2020, 2022, against 2018, 2019, 2022 on the old design and 2016, 2018, 2019, 2022 on the new model with the endorsed gauges.
  • Levels. The re-selected gauges carry more water: 1-in-4 levels of 3,702–4,341 m³/s, against 3,462–3,556 for the endorsed main-channel gauges on the new model. The small tributary gauge of the endorsed set drops out.
Only the gauge selection is re-run. The return period (1-in-4), the gauge count (6 of 10) and the lead window were chosen on the old model by the design's trigger assessment, which is not re-run here. The candidate pool is the one the design drew from: the 42 Google gauges Flood Hub listed in the seven target LGAs, plus GloFAS at Wuroboki. When Google switches Flood Hub to the new model, its list of gauges may change too.

6Method

  • Selection as in the design's workflow notebooks 01, 03 and 04: the stored candidate pool; each Google candidate's best Spearman correlation with FloodScan (August–November 1998–2023, lags of −3 to +14 days); all candidates ranked together, top ten kept. GloFAS at Wuroboki keeps its stored correlation (0.679), as its series does not depend on the Google model.
  • Run on the old model, the same code reproduces the stored correlations of all 42 Google candidates exactly and selects the ten endorsed gauges.
  • Trigger and scores with the comparison page's code (analysis.py); this variant in reselect.py.
