Nigeria flooding — anticipatory action / operational note

The new Google model and the Adamawa action trigger

Google has released a new version of the flood model behind Flood Hub (November 2026). The 2026 action trigger runs on Google: at least 6 of 10 gauges on the Benue in Adamawa over their own 1-in-4 level on the same day. This page replays it on the new model with exactly the method used to design it on the old one: the same ten gauges, the same August–November season, the same 1998–2023 calibration years, the same level and gauge count. Only the per-gauge levels are refitted, on the new model's own record. The readiness trigger runs on GloFAS and does not change. Analysis of 4 November 2026.

1What changes

  • Activation years: none. Both models meet the rule in 1998, 1999, 2012, 2018, 2019, 2022: 1-in-4.5, as in the framework. Scores against FloodScan are therefore identical (section 5).
  • Timing within the season does change. The first day the rule is met moves by 1 day earlier in 1998, 21 days later in 2012, 24 days earlier in 2019, 6 days earlier in 2022 (same day in 1999, 2018). The two large moves are 2012, which the new model reaches 21 days after the old model and after FloodScan's first day over its own 1-in-4 level, and 2019, which it reaches 24 days earlier.
  • Forecasts (archive 2016–2022 on both models, leads 0–7 days): the old model's forecasts meet the rule in 2018, 2019, 2022, the new model's in 2016, 2018, 2019, 2022. 2016 is new: a forecast issued 23 Sep put 9 of 10 gauges over their level, while neither model's record nor FloodScan show a 1-in-4 flood that year. The first qualifying forecast moves by 11 days later in 2018, 11 days earlier in 2019, 3 days earlier in 2022.
  • Levels. The new model runs about three times higher at these gauges: 1-in-4 levels of 3,462–3,556 m³/s against 1,102–1,117 on the nine main-channel gauges. Its August–November flows follow FloodScan slightly more closely (best correlation 0.73–0.76 against 0.71–0.74), and match it best with no time offset on the main channel, where the old model's best match was offset by three days.
  • Same years throughout. Every old-versus-new comparison on this page uses only the years both versions cover: the record 1998–2023 (the calibration years) and, for forecasts, the seasons 2016–2022 that both archives cover completely. What the new model says about 2023–2025 beyond that is in section 6: the rule is not met in 2024 or 2025.
  • Gauges. This page keeps the ten endorsed gauges and refits only their levels. A variant page also re-runs the design's gauge selection on the new model and applies the rule to the ten gauges it picks.
Before Google switches Flood Hub to the new model. On 4 November 2026 the Flood Hub API still serves the old model: Wuroboki's warning, danger and extreme-danger levels are unchanged (1,094, 1,352 and 1,648 m³/s) and gauges keep their hybas_ identifiers. The monitoring reads Google against the endorsed levels (about 1,100–1,120 m³/s on the main channel). The new model's ordinary wet-season flows sit far above those levels: applied to the new record they would have met the rule in every one of the 26 seasons 1998–2023 (and in 2024 and 2025 too). The levels have to be replaced by the refitted ones (section 4) when the API switches, and before that if Google announces a date. Signals that the switch has happened: the warning levels or gauge model identifiers change for these gauges, or identifiers move from hybas_ to subhybas_ as in the new dataset (not confirmed).

7Method

  • The design notebook's steps (exploration/2026/cerf/workflow/06_trigger_definition.ipynb) run on each model's data in turn, in analysis.py next to this page: the estimator is copied verbatim; a gauge counts when strictly above its level; forecast valid day = issue day + lead, leads 0–7, best value per day across issues, years whose forecasts reach October.
  • The old model run here reproduces the endorsed levels to 0.1 m³/s and the endorsed years (record 1998, 1999, 2012, 2018, 2019, 2022; forecast 2018, 2019, 2022).
  • Same years for both versions: levels fitted on 1998–2023 for both; activations, timing, FloodScan scores, correlations and the old-levels-on-new-flows count all over 1998–2023; forecasts over the complete seasons 2016–2022.
  • The gauge set is the endorsed one; gauge selection is re-run on the new model in the variant page. FloodScan benchmark years are the design's.
  • Data: Google Runoff Reanalysis & Reforecast, public Google Cloud bucket flood-forecasting, old version model_id_8583a5c2_v0, new version distributed_model_2026_11 (CC-BY-4.0).
